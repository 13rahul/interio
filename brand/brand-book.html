From a word to a world.

Graphic designers do not start with a logo. They start with the business, the people it serves, and the culture it lives in. For a Dubai studio serving Emirati and Arab families, that means hospitality, privacy, heritage craft, and a bilingual identity from day one.

01 · Discover — The name

o·pu·lent liv·ing

noun phrase

A way of life marked by great wealth, lavish comfort, grand aesthetics, and an abundance of fine things.

After the Cambridge and Merriam-Webster dictionaries.

Lavish display

Art, custom architecture, and pieces that are meant to be seen.

Rich materials

Marble, gold accents, crystal chandeliers, and plush textures.

Abundance

More than enough: options, comfort, and detail beyond the basic.

The tension we design for

The name promises opulence. The founder promises affordable luxury.

In Arabian culture, generosity to the guest (karam) is the truest wealth. The brand keeps the grandeur of the majlis and drops the excess: grand on the eye, honest on the invoice.

Concept 01 · Al Bab الباب

The Majlis Gate

The Palm Crest

06 · Guidelines — Colour

Wealthy green, gold, and beige.

Use roughly 60% beige grounds, 30% green, and 10% gold. Gold is an accent: a line, a monogram, a foil, or the lattice pattern. It is never a large flat background.

08 · Guidelines — Brand pattern

The mashrabiya lattice.

An eight-point khatam star repeated into a screen, the same lattice that fills the arch of the logo. Use it at low opacity on green, as foil on packaging, laser-cut in brass or timber, and as the lining of envelopes and boxes.

09 · Guidelines — Using the logo

Space to breathe.

Clear space

Keep a margin equal to the medallion's width on every side. Nothing enters it.

Minimum size

Full bilingual logo no smaller than 25 mm wide in print and 140 px on screen. Below that, use the gate emblem alone.
